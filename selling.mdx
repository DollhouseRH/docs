## You can always sell

There is no sell tax, no lock-up, no cooldown and no seasoning period on any coin in Dollhouse. Buys and sells pay the same 1%.

Design decision: there is no sell lock, and there never will be.

## Selling a coin

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the coin and the amount">
    You can sell back to $DOLL, or stop part-way and hold the parent coin instead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the quote">
    The route runs back up the chain: your coin into its parent, its parent into its parent, and finally into $DOLL. Selling $DOLL itself for ETH happens on Pons.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    One transaction.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you pay

**1% on the way out**, charged once on the $DOLL side of link one's pool. This is the same 1% as on the way in, charged once each direction, not per hop.

**7.5 basis points per family hop** on the way back up.

**Price impact**, which again is the bigger number on a deep route.

If you sell only as far as the parent coin and hold it, you pay the hop fees and no 1% at all, because your money never left link one.

## Who is buying

The pool is. Every coin's liquidity is locked permanently, so the curve keeps bidding all the way down and cannot be withdrawn mid-fall.

As the price falls, the pool hands back the parent coin it absorbed on the way up. That is a real bid, and it is also the only one you are guaranteed.

<Warning>
Locked liquidity is not a floor. A curve that bids all the way down still lets the price fall a long way. A dump of half the free float takes a link down hard even with reinforcement running, and the protocol has **no pause, no circuit breaker and no rescue**.
</Warning>

## Selling a loser

A coin that lost its round stays tradable forever. The router still walks the path through the coin it was paired with during its round.

Its creator still collects the creator share on those trades, at 100% after the round is over.

<Warning>
After a round ends, a loser's market is thinner than a canonical coin's, and its route is only supported through the parent it was registered against. Expect worse execution than on the trunk.
</Warning>

## One mechanical detail

Fees are charged on the amount you **requested**, not on the amount that actually filled. If a route only fills half your order, you still pay the fee on the whole request. Size your orders accordingly.
