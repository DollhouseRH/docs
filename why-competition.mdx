## What you gain from the competition

Every coin in the chain has earned its spot. The head coin becomes the money that every buyer in the next round has to hold, so a market-judged competition decides who gets that position, not a race to launch first or an insider allowlist.

**A coin that loses still keeps trading.** Its pool never closes, and its creator keeps earning on it, just without a share of the chain's future fees.

**The winner becomes the parent everyone in the next round prices in.** Every candidate in that round is bought and sold against it, which pulls real buying into it for the whole round, win or lose.

## How to take part

Anyone can enter a coin. There is no allowlist and no approval step. **Buying a candidate during a round is how you vote for it**, and selling takes your vote back.

Rounds run for a fixed length, from fifteen minutes early on up to twelve hours, and the score is the average market cap over the closing window: the last fifteen minutes of the round. See [how a round works](/how-a-round-works).

## The minimum bar

Winning the round is not enough on its own. The leader also has to absorb an average of **0.15% of the parent coin's supply** across the closing window. If nobody clears it, the round ends with no winner, every bond in it is forfeited, and the bar drops for the next attempt until somebody clears it.

Even a round with no winner still pulls real buying into the head coin, since every candidate is priced in it.
