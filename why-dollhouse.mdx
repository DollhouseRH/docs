## Why buy here

**You are early by design.** Every round starts new coins at the very bottom of their curve. There is no private round and no team allocation on any candidate coin. \$DOLL itself is adopted from Pons, where early buyers bought at the same open as everyone else. See [The \$DOLL coin](/the-genesis-coin).

**One fee, at the door.** You pay 1% when your $DOLL enters the chain and 1% when it leaves. Moving between coins inside the chain costs a small pool fee per hop. The 1% never repeats, however deep the chain goes.

**The liquidity cannot be pulled.** Every coin's entire supply goes into its pool at launch and the pool is locked forever. There is no owner function that can remove it. A creator cannot rug the pool because there is nothing for them to rug.

**Your buys do something upstream.** Buying a coin means buying its parent first, which means buying its parent's parent, and so on up to \$DOLL. That is a real trade on every pool in between, not a fee rebate. Getting ETH into \$DOLL in the first place happens on Pons.

<Warning>
None of this stops a coin from going to zero. Locked liquidity means the pool keeps bidding on the way down; it does not mean the price holds. See [Risks](/risks).
</Warning>

## Why launch here

**Permissionless.** Anyone can enter a coin. There is no allowlist, no signature from a team wallet, and no approval step. See [Why a competition](/why-competition).

**You keep 40% of the trading fee, forever.** Not for a season, not until some migration. The split is a constant in the contract and cannot be changed by anyone.

**Losing is not fatal.** A coin that loses a round keeps its pool, keeps trading, and its creator keeps earning the creator share on it. It simply does not join the chain.

**Winning earns you a season.** While your coin is the head of the chain, every candidate in the next round is priced in *your* coin, and you take half of the creator share on all of that candidate trading. That is the "season cut."

**Costs.** You pay gas and a refundable bond. The bond comes back in full if you win. It is forfeited if you lose. See [Launching a coin](/launching-a-coin).

## What the rest of the fee buys you

**A demand pump for your coin if you win.** When your coin is the head, the next round's candidates are all priced in it. A round pulls meaningful buying into the head coin, and that effect barely moves whether 5 or 50 candidates compete.

**A permanent share of everything built after you.** 20% of the fee is split among every earlier coin in the chain, forever, as locked buy-side liquidity under those coins.

**A flat fee that does not grow with depth.** The 1% is charged once, however deep the chain gets, so buying deep into the chain does not cost more than buying near the top.

**A quality filter.** Your coin is only in the chain if the market put it there. That is the point of [the competition](/why-competition).

<Note>
"Your buys are your votes." There is no separate voting token and no governance poll. During a round, buying a candidate is how you vote for it.
</Note>
