## Supply

Every candidate coin on Dollhouse has a fixed supply of **1,000,000,000 tokens**. It is minted once at launch. There is no mint function afterwards.

\$DOLL is not launched by Dollhouse, so its supply and allocation are decided entirely by Pons, not by this protocol. Trading \$DOLL for ETH on Pons pays Pons's own 1% pool fee, part of which buys back \$DOLL and locks it in a vault that releases over five years. See [The \$DOLL coin](/the-genesis-coin).

## The full journey

| Step | Fee | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Trading \$DOLL for ETH, on Pons | Pons's 1% pool fee, plus a creator tax fixed at launch | 30% to Pons, 70% to a creator bucket: half cash to the \$DOLL creator, half spent buying back \$DOLL on its own pool, locked in a vault and released over 5 years |
| Entering the chain from \$DOLL | 1% once, on the \$DOLL side of link one, in both directions | Split as below; suppressed for the first 3 seconds of a new round-one pool, while the snipe tax applies |
| Each hop deeper in the chain | 0.075% per hop, in the parent coin | The hop's parent coin, as reinforcement |
| The 1%'s split | 40% / 20% / 20% / 20% | Creator, developer, earlier coins, parent reinforcement |

## Where the supply goes

**Candidate coins: 100% into the locked pool.** No creator allocation, no team allocation, no reserve, no airdrop. Everything is liquidity from the first second.

There is no pre-allocation anywhere in Dollhouse. Early buyers get \$DOLL at the Pons open like everyone else, and there is no unlock contract on any coin.

## The trading fee

**1%, charged once on the \$DOLL side of link one's pool, in both directions.** Once on the way in, once on the way out. It does not increase with depth and it is not charged on family-to-family pools.

There is no sell tax. Buys and sells pay the same 1%.

**7.5 basis points (0.075%) per family-to-family hop**, paid in the parent coin, which goes into that parent's reinforcement budget.

**The snipe tax:** on a candidate's first 3 seconds of trading, a tax starting at 99% and falling to 1%. Second by second in practice it works out to roughly 99%, 66%, 34%, then zero from +3 seconds. On a new round-one pool, the edge fee is suppressed for that same 3-second window and the snipe tax applies instead, so the two never stack past 100%. See [Risks](/risks).

## How the 1% is split

| Share | Recipient | Delivered as |
|---|---|---|
| 40% | Creator | \$DOLL, pull-claimed |
| 20% | Developer | \$DOLL, pull-claimed |
| 20% | Ancestor sleeve: every earlier coin | Locked buy-side liquidity, never cash |
| 20% | Immediate parent reinforcement | Locked buy-side liquidity, never cash |

**The season split.** During a round, a candidate's attributed trades split the 40% creator share **50/50**: 20% of the fee to the candidate's creator, 20% to the creator of the head coin the round is priced in. After the round ends, 100% of the creator share on that coin goes to its own creator.

## How ancestors are paid

The ancestor share is divided among all earlier coins by a U-shaped weight: oldest coins highest, middle coins lowest, newest coins in between.

\$DOLL is always worth exactly **2x the newest link**, by construction, and never more. There is no fixed floor on \$DOLL's own share, so it shrinks as the chain grows: 34.5% of the ancestor sleeve at 5 links, 0.24% at 1,000 links.

The money is never paid out as cash. It buys parent tokens and locks them as a permanent bid under the ancestor's pool, and a volunteer keeper does the conversion for a 1% bounty. Draws are capped at 10% of an entitlement per 24 hours and 2% of the active liquidity range per call. Generation 0 and 1's pots deploy as locked \$DOLL buy support under link one directly; deeper generations go through the conversion above.

How much \$DOLL a deployment spends is worked out along the chain, each step taken at the lowest of the current price, the 30-minute average and the 7-day average. Where the bid is then placed is decided by the receiving pool itself: its current price has to be within about 3% of its own 30-minute average before anything is placed, and the bid sits immediately beneath its live price.

## Where a generation's purse goes

A generation is the winner of a round plus every coin that lost the same round. The generation's share of later fees, its **purse**, is not divided between them.

All of it is locked as bid liquidity **under the coin that won the round**, at every deployment, forever. There is nothing measured afterwards, nothing to rank and nothing to claim: the round decides it, and the round is over.

It is locked as a **permanent buy wall under that coin**: a standing bid from just under the current price down to about 6% below it, never withdrawn, which the coin's own holders can sell into at any time. Each deployment is capped at 2% of the pool's depth, and at most 10% of that round's accrued share may be deployed per 24 hours.

Coins that lost the round keep their own pools and their own creator fees, and they trade forever. They receive no purse.

## The bond

Entering a round requires a refundable bond, paid in \$DOLL: approve the token, then register.

On mainnet the bond is flat: the same amount at every chain depth, spam resistance only. It is set at deployment from \$DOLL's graduated Pons price, to roughly twenty dollars at launch. Testnet uses a smaller, depth-scaled schedule instead, to exercise that path of the contract.

| | Base | Schedule |
|---|---|---|
| Testnet | a small amount of MockDoll | doubles every 4 links, capped at a fixed ceiling |
| Mainnet | a flat amount in \$DOLL, set at deployment to roughly twenty dollars at launch | flat: no doubling |

The round pins its bond when it opens, and every candidate stores what it paid, so the refund and the forfeit always use the actual amount posted.

**Winners get the whole bond back.** Only losers forfeit, and those forfeited bonds become locked \$DOLL buy support under link one.

<Note>
The bond is refundable capital, not a price. Winners always get it back in full.
</Note>

## The curves

Every candidate coin sells its supply along a fixed multi-range curve, set in the contract, identical in shape for every coin. The four ranges take 20%, 25%, 35% and 20% of the supply on sale, across valuation bands that run from one thousandth of the parent's supply value up to 100x it, with the last range as a tail.

\$DOLL's own curve was Pons's, not Dollhouse's: it ran on Pons's bonding curve and graduated into a Uniswap v4 pool under Pons's hook before Dollhouse ever adopted it. See [The \$DOLL coin](/the-genesis-coin).

By the time a winning coin has cleared the bar, roughly 38% of its supply has been sold with about 11% of embedded backing.

## What the developer can and cannot do

<Accordion title="Can">
- Claim their own accrued 20% fee share, in \$DOLL, to an address they choose.
- Transfer the developer fee address on a public 7-day delay.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Cannot">
- Remove liquidity from any pool. No such function exists, for anyone.
- Change the fee, the split, the curve, the threshold, the round timings or the bond.
- Choose or overturn a round's winner.
- Rewrite the chain's history. Every canonical entry is write-once.
- Pause trading, blacklist an address, or freeze a balance.
- Mint more of any coin.
- Take anything from a candidate coin's supply. Candidates have no allocation at all.
</Accordion>

<Warning>
One more disclosure on the developer address. The fee ledger is a single balance, so whatever has already accrued is claimable by whoever holds the developer role **at claim time**. Transferring the role hands over the unclaimed balance too.
</Warning>

## The steward

A separate address, the **steward**, has exactly two powers and no others: announce a sunset that names a successor deployment, and cancel that announcement before it takes effect. It cannot touch a pool, a price, a fee, a winner or a balance.

On mainnet the steward is planned as a **single cold hardware-wallet signer**, not a multisig. Any transfer of the role goes through a public 7-day delay.

<Warning>
A single signer is a single point of failure. If that key is lost, the sunset path is gone forever. If it is stolen, the thief gets 7 days of public notice before they hold the role, and the sunset switch itself is a serious power. See [Risks](/risks).
</Warning>

## Rules that protect your money

- **Liquidity locked forever.** Every coin's full supply goes into its pool at launch, and the pool can never be emptied, by anyone, including the developer.
- **The purse is a permanent buy wall.** Every coin's share of fees from everything launched after it is locked as a standing bid under the coin that won its round, forever, and can never be withdrawn.
- **Losing bonds become buy support, not a fee for anyone.** A forfeited bond is delivered as locked \$DOLL buy support under link one when the round closes.
- **The snipe tax prices out the opening block.** The first 3 seconds of a candidate's trading carry a tax starting at 99% and falling to 1%, so being first in the block is not free money.
- **The round ends at a random moment.** A public randomness beacon decides the exact closing second inside the last 3 minutes of a round, so nobody can time a guaranteed last-second buy.
- **Nobody can change any of these rules.** There is no dial, no admin switch and no vote that can move the fee, the split, the curve, the schedule or a round's result.

See [Risks](/risks) for what can still go wrong.
