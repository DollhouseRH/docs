## One line of paired coins

Dollhouse has one chain. Every coin in it is paired with the coin before it, called its parent. The newest coin is the head coin, and each round, a timed contest between new coins, adds the next coin below it.

```text
(ETH) → $DOLL → #1 → #2 → #3 → #4 → ...
```

Coin #3's pool holds #2, #2's pool holds #1, and #1's pool holds \$DOLL. No pool in Dollhouse holds ETH. \$DOLL is bought with ETH in its own market outside Dollhouse. The chain starts at link one, the pool between \$DOLL and coin #1.

A coin's price is quoted in its parent. When a coin's price moves, traders who buy in one pool and sell in another carry that move up to every coin above it.

A coin that wins its round joins the chain and gets a numbered spot. Its number and parent are written on-chain once and never changed. While it is the head, the coins entered in the next round, called candidates, are priced in it. From then on, it earns a part of the chain share, the part of each fee locked into the chain, on every coin launched after it.

## Buy walls from every pool

Every pool keeps 0.075% of each trade, in the coin that pool is priced in. That money becomes a [buy wall](/the-flywheel) just under the chain coin priced in that same coin. For a coin in the chain, that is its own pool. So the more a coin trades, the more buying sits under it.

Candidates in a round are all priced in the head coin. Their 0.075% goes under whichever coin wins that round.

Buy walls are placed in portions over several days, not all at once. When sellers fill a buy wall, the coins it buys stay locked in the pool too. Nothing ever leaves the pool.

## The chain share

The chain share is 40% of the 1% fee, and it is never paid as cash. Half of it goes under every earlier coin, from \$DOLL down to the traded coin's parent. Earlier coins share it unevenly, and \$DOLL always gets about twice the newest coin's part.

The other half goes under the traded coin's parent. During a round, every candidate's parent is the head coin. So trading candidates builds buy walls under the head.

Each coin's part becomes a buy wall in that coin's own pool, just under its price. A round's part always goes to the coin that won that round. The other coins from that round get none of it.
