## What $DOLL is

\$DOLL is not launched by Dollhouse. It is created on **Pons**, a separate launchpad on Robinhood Chain: a bonding curve that graduates into a Uniswap v4 pool quoted in ETH, under Pons's own hook and locked by Pons's own locker. Dollhouse **adopts** that token as its entrance coin once, as part of the one-time deployment step, with the attribution fixed at deployment, so whoever finishes deploying the contracts, the outcome is identical, and there is nothing to race for. There is no Dollhouse-run curve for \$DOLL, no insider allocation on it, and no unlock contract; early buyers get it at the Pons open like everyone else.

Name: **Dollhouse**. Symbol: **$DOLL**. Supply: 1,000,000,000. It is the outermost nesting doll: link zero, the coin every other coin is ultimately priced against. It has no pool inside Dollhouse itself; its market is the one it graduated into on Pons.

<Note>
The testnet deployments used a placeholder token in place of \$DOLL, called **MockDoll**. Mainnet will adopt the real **\$DOLL** from Pons. Nothing is deployed on mainnet yet.
</Note>

## Why it is structurally different from the other coins

**It is the door.** Every trade that brings \$DOLL into the chain, or takes it back out at link one, is where the 1% edge fee is charged, in both directions. There is no other entrance inside Dollhouse. Getting ETH into \$DOLL itself, or out of it, happens on Pons and is outside Dollhouse's accounting entirely.

**Every round's demand flows down through it.** To buy a candidate you need the head coin. To get the head coin you need its parent. Follow that all the way up and you are buying $DOLL. A round that pulls meaningful head-coin demand pulls it through here.

**Ancestor payouts weight it highest.** $DOLL sits at the oldest end of the U-shaped weight curve and receives exactly **2x the newest link's share** of the ancestor sleeve, permanently, at every depth.

**Losers' bonds land under it.** Bonds are posted in \$DOLL. Every forfeited bond is delivered as locked \$DOLL buy support underneath link one when the round closes; if that delivery ever fails, the round still closes and the bond is delivered later, by anyone. So are the edge fees \$DOLL's own crossings earn.

**Its liquidity can never be pulled, but Dollhouse does not control it.** \$DOLL's pool lives on Pons, locked by Pons's locker under Pons's own rules; Dollhouse has no allocation of its own on \$DOLL and no function that touches that pool.

## What that adds up to, in measured terms

Over 20 generations at \$250,000 of edge volume per round, the model puts a meaningful amount of ancestor sleeve plus forfeited bonds under \$DOLL as locked bid liquidity.

That is real and it is modest. It is delivered as protocol-owned buy support, not as a payout to holders.

## Now the counter-case

<Warning>
**$DOLL can go to zero.** It is a meme coin on a launchpad. Locked liquidity means the pool keeps bidding all the way down; it does not put a floor under the price. Nothing here is a promise of value, appreciation or income.
</Warning>

**The support arrives slowly and can be released on the way down.** Ancestor bids are rate-limited to 10% of an entitlement per 24 hours and 2% of a range per call. Reinforcement arrives slowly and is not a shock absorber; it barely changes the depth of a large dump. And a locked bid that gets hit is spent, the curve hands its holdings back to sellers as the price falls. It is not a ratchet.

**The payouts depend on volunteers.** Converting the ancestor sleeve into locked bids is done by keepers for a bounty, and at measured sizes keepers **lose money** doing it. If nobody calls, an entitlement simply sits there.

**Pons is a hard dependency for getting in or out.** Dollhouse never mints or prices \$DOLL itself; the only way to convert ETH into \$DOLL, or back, is Pons's own pool. If Pons is unavailable, the chain still runs internally, but it has no door.

**Genesis's share shrinks as the chain grows.** By design there is no genesis floor. Its cut of the ancestor sleeve is 34.5% at 5 links and 0.24% at 1,000.

**Everything upstream depends on activity downstream.** If rounds stop happening, the flywheel stops. There is no treasury, no buyback, and no subsidy to restart it.

**The chain loses \$DOLL value with depth.** Each link has measured at roughly 5–8% of its parent's value, so deep coins are small in \$DOLL terms and the demand they push upward is correspondingly small.

**The upgrade switch is a real risk to $DOLL specifically.** Link one's pool is the one that charges the 1% edge fee. If a sunset takes effect, that fee is booked by a successor deployment instead. See [Risks](/risks).

## How to think about it

$DOLL's case is structural, not promissory: it is the entrance, it is weighted highest in the payouts, and its liquidity cannot be removed.

Whether any of that translates into price depends entirely on whether people keep launching and trading here. Nobody, including the developer, can make that happen.
